Migration attitudes are usually studied through local exposure: migrant population size, regional concentration, refugee arrivals, or direct contact. In an increasingly connected world, this paper asks whether cross-border digital connectedness helps explain why some countries are more accepting of migrants than others, whether this relationship also holds between regions within the same country, and whether digital connectedness amplifies or substitutes for offline contact. We link the Facebook Social Connectedness Index to migrant-acceptance measures from the Gallup World Poll, the European Social Survey, and the Eurobarometer. Across four Gallup waves, countries with stronger cross-border connectedness are consistently more accepting of migrants. The regional patterns are weaker and less stable, suggesting that much of the association operates between countries rather than within them. Offline contact is generally associated with higher acceptance, but its interaction with social connectedness varies across datasets. Our results broaden how migrant exposure is measured and understood, showing that migrant acceptance can also reflect cross-border social ties.
